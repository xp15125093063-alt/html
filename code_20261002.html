A Historic Victory: The First Female Esports Champion at the Asian Games

On the evening of September 30, 2026, at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games in Japan, a remarkable chapter was written in the history of electronic sports. China's national Identity V (Asian Games Version) team defeated the host Japanese team 2–0 in the final, claiming the first-ever gold medal for this event since it was officially included in the Asian Games. This victory also marked China's 151st gold medal of the Games. However, what truly captured the world's attention was not merely the scoreline — it was the 20-year-old woman who stood at the center of the celebration.
Mu Xinyue, known by her in-game ID "Persica" (桃晚安), became the first female athlete ever to win a gold medal in an Asian Games esports event. In a competitive landscape overwhelmingly dominated by male players, her achievement carries significance that extends far beyond a single trophy.
Identity V is an asymmetrical multiplayer game — a format unique in the Asian Games esports lineup. Each match pits one "Hunter" against four "Survivors," creating an inherent imbalance that demands precise coordination, split-second decision-making, and psychological resilience from every participant. Mu, playing as a Survivor, was the only woman on China's seven-member squad. Yet throughout the tournament, she delivered performances that silenced every doubt.
The road to the final was anything but smooth. On the decisive day, China had to overcome South Korea and Chinese Taipei in succession before facing Japan, whose players enjoyed the overwhelming support of a home crowd. Under such intense pressure, Mu's composure became the team's anchor. In the crucial final round, with the match hanging in the balance, she controlled the character "Perfumer" and executed a flawless escape through the cellar — a move that sealed the victory and left even the veteran commentators gasping in admiration. Over the full five maps played across the best-of-three series, Mu committed not a single critical error.
What makes her story even more compelling is the journey behind it. Born in 2006 in Jinan, Shandong Province, Mu left home at 16 to pursue a career in professional gaming — a decision her family did not initially support. Living alone, she supported herself by boosting accounts for other players, a grueling routine that tested both her skill and her determination. At 18, she signed with the Beijing WBG Identity V division and quickly made her mark on the professional league (IVL), earning the "Rookie of the Year" award. Her signature character, the "Mechanic," became synonymous with her name in the Chinese competitive scene. Yet the path was not without setbacks: in the 2025 IVL Summer Split, her team reached the grand final only to finish as runners-up. When asked about the Asian Games a year ago, she reportedly said, "I probably won't even get to go."
She did go. And she won.
Esports was granted full medal status at the 2022 Hangzhou Asian Games, but female representation remained scarce. Of the hundreds of registered esports athletes across all events in Nagoya, only a tiny fraction were women. Mu's gold medal challenges the long-standing stereotype that competitive gaming is inherently a "male domain." As one commentator put it, "The Mechanic's traits are fragility and timidity — but Persica's courage is beyond question."
When the Chinese national anthem played in the arena in Tokoname, and the gold medal was placed around her neck, Mu Xinyue did not merely win a game. She rewrote what was considered possible for women in esports — proving that on the digital battlefield, skill knows no gender.
